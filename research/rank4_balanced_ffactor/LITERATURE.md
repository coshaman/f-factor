# Literature freeze (28 August 2026)

The primary source is Isabel Beckenbach and Robert Scheidweiler,
“Perfect f-matchings and f-factors in hypergraphs—A combinatorial
approach,” *Discrete Mathematics* 340(10), 2499–2506 (2017), DOI
[10.1016/j.disc.2017.05.005](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.disc.2017.05.005).
Its complexity theorem states NP-completeness for f-factors and perfect
f-matchings in non-uniform balanced hypergraphs, using a 3-dimensional
matching reduction. The associated dissertation gives the construction
explicitly and states that the rank-at-least-five reduction leaves rank four
as the only open rank case.

The dissertation and the journal article use the strong-cycle definition of
balancedness and define an f-factor as a 0/1 edge subset. A perfect
f-matching is an integer nonnegative edge multiplicity vector. Balanced
hypergraphs of rank at most three have totally unimodular incidence matrices,
so both systems are polynomially decidable by linear programming; the
non-uniform rank-at-least-five hardness construction is the prior boundary.

Searches on 28 August 2026 covered the title, DOI, author names, rank-four
and balanced-incidence terminology in DBLP, arXiv-indexed results, publisher
pages, Crossref-style results, and citation-oriented web indexes. The
results located no later paper proving either rank-four target theorem.
Recent papers returned by broad searches concern dense uniform hypergraph
matchings, ordinary graph f-factors, or unrelated hypergraph factors, and do
not apply to balanced non-uniform rank-four incidence systems.

This is a documented search result, not a claim that an inaccessible or
unindexed manuscript cannot exist. The candidate construction below is
therefore treated as a new proof subject to the independent audits, not as
an assumed literature fact.
